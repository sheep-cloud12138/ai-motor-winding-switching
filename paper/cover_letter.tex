\begin{letter}{Editor-in-Chief\\Advanced Engineering Informatics}

\opening{Dear Editor,}

Please find enclosed our manuscript ``Coverage-controlled learning of
connection-invariant magnetic surrogates for reconfigurable electric
machines'' for consideration as a research paper in Advanced Engineering
Informatics.

Reconfigurable windings are an established route to wide-speed-range,
high-efficiency electrified powertrains, yet candidate connections are
usually evaluated with dedicated FE models or flux-map tables. Our
manuscript addresses this with one idea: a winding
connection is a set of linear constraints on coil currents, so a single
connection-invariant surrogate---trained once on coil-level FE data---can
query unseen connections; coverage determines when zero-shot transfer is
reliable. We think this fits the journal's
scope in three specific ways: (i) it is an engineering-informatics method
(learned surrogates embedded in design and operation workflows) rather than
a new machine or a new controller; (ii) it comes with a reproducible
benchmark---seven hold-out connections, three difficulty tiers, 9{,}276+
nonlinear FE samples and fixed splits---released with code and data; and
(iii) it delivers actionable engineering findings, including two
data-collection principles, a quantified physical-consistency advantage of
co-energy surrogates, and an honest negative drive-cycle result. A
105-point voltage/gear sweep found no case in which reconfiguration
created feasibility, and the nominal WLTC schedule saved only 0.008\,\%.
An independent FEMM cross-check limits the claims explicitly, while
supporting end-to-end torque $R^2=0.89$--$0.92$ on three unseen
connections.

All results in the manuscript are reproducible from the released code and
data with one command per figure; the FE reference data, trained models, and
benchmark splits are included. The work has not been published elsewhere and
is not under consideration elsewhere. The authors declare no competing
interests.
