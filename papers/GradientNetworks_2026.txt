Gradient Networks for Universal Magnetic
Modeling of Synchronous Machines
Junyi Li, Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Tim Foißner, Floran Martin,
Antti Piippo, and Marko Hinkkanen, Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—This paper presents a physics-constrained neural
network framework for magnetic modeling of saturable syn-
chronous machines, including spatial harmonics. By embed-
ding gradient networks into the machine equations to model
conservative electromagnetic behavior, the framework satisfies
reciprocity and energy conservation by construction, while uni-
versally approximating any physically feasible magnetic charac-
teristic. Unlike lookup tables and black-box neural networks, it
guarantees monotonicity, invertibility, and smooth outputs, and
remains highly data efficient. The method is validated using
measured and finite-element method (FEM) data from a 5.6-kW
permanent-magnet (PM) synchronous reluctance machine, and is
demonstrated in real-time closed-loop control on an embedded
platform. The results confirm accurate, physically consistent, and
computationally efficient performance.
Index Terms—Dynamic modeling, electric drives, Hamiltonian,
magnetic saturation, neural networks, physics-informed machine
learning, synchronous machines.
I. Introduction
D
YNAMIC models of synchronous machines are essential
for control, estimation, monitoring, and optimization. A
particularly challenging aspect is the magnetic model, which
describes the relationship between flux linkages, currents,
rotor angle, and electromagnetic torque [1], [2]. In modern
power-dense designs, such as permanent-magnet (PM) and
synchronous reluctance machines, magnetic saturation and
cross-saturation effects are significant and must often be incor-
porated into control models. Spatial harmonics, on the other
hand, can be included in high-fidelity models for time-domain
simulations during the design stage.
The nonlinear magnetic behavior is typically represented
by analytical functions [3]–[7], semi-analytical models [8],
lookup tables [9]–[13], or neural networks [14]–[18]. These
models can be characterized from finite-element method
(FEM) data [19], laboratory measurements [13], [20], or
automatic identification routines [21]. Analytical models are
limited by their functional form and are difficult to extend to
higher dimensions (e.g., for spatial harmonics or multi-phase
machines). Lookup tables suffer from the curse of dimen-
sionality, high memory requirements, and non-smooth outputs

require less memory than lookup tables, but they require large
training datasets and do not guarantee reciprocity [22] or
invertibility. Although some architectures incorporate spatial
periodicity [14], [18], neither reciprocity nor monotonicity is
guaranteed by construction.
Physics-informed neural networks address these limitations
by embedding known physical principles [23]–[26]. Electric
machines are port-Hamiltonian systems [27], in which the
current and electromagnetic torque are gradients of the mag-
netic field energy with respect to the flux linkage and rotor
angle, respectively [1], [2]. Hamiltonian neural networks [25],
[26] could exploit this structure by modeling the energy as
a scalar network and differentiating it, which improves data
efficiency and physical consistency. However, they become
harder to optimize as the input dimension grows, lack a
universal approximation guarantee for the gradient field [28],
and require second-order backpropagation in training and
network differentiation at inference. A similar differentiation
principle was applied to electromagnetic torque modeling [18],
but the relationship between currents and flux linkages was not
considered.
In this paper, we propose a physics-constrained magnetic
modeling framework that embeds gradient networks [28] into
the fundamental electromechanical equations of synchronous
machines [1], [2]. Gradient networks directly model monotone
gradient fields without differentiating a scalar network, avoid-
ing these training and inference costs. The main contributions
are:
• an energy-based magnetic modeling framework in which
current and torque are derived from the gradient of a field
energy function, guaranteeing reciprocity and monotonic-
ity (and thus invertibility) by construction;
• a co-energy-based formulation for directly modeling flux
linkage and torque with the same physical guarantees;
• architectural encodings of physical structure, enforcing q-
axis symmetry via energy symmetrization and capturing
spatial harmonics via Fourier features [29];
• a computationally efficient 𝑝-norm gradient activation,
together with guidance for selecting activation functions
for current and flux-linkage maps;
• experimental validation on two PM synchronous reluc-
tance machines (5.6 and 28 kW) alongside FEM valida-
tion, demonstrating data efficiency and real-time deploy-
ment in a closed-loop embedded drive system.

C. Application Examples
To demonstrate the practical applicability of the proposed
approach, the learned magnetic models are evaluated in high-
fidelity simulations and experimentally on a real-time closed-
loop drive system. Fig. 6 shows the experimental setup, where
the same 5.6-kW PM synchronous reluctance machine is
controlled by a dSPACE MicroLabBox.
The controller is a flux-vector control scheme [33], [34]
parameterized by the proposed co-energy-based model (6)
without spatial harmonics [𝑝-norm gradient activation (14)
with 𝑝= 8, 𝑁= 6]. The control system comprehensively
employs the proposed model in its control law, flux observer,
and online reference generation. Consequently, the model is
evaluated multiple times during each 12-kHz control cycle.
Notably, the applied online reference generation method [35]
numerically inverts the flux-linkage map to obtain the current
for the maximum-torque-per-volt (MTPV) calculation, which

1) High-Fidelity Simulation: In the simulation, the machine
is represented by the proposed energy-based magnetic model
(7) with spatial harmonics (softmax activation, 𝑁= 48),
trained on the FEM dataset as described in Section IV-B.
This model variant is convenient since the flux linkages are
the state variables of the voltage equations (4). Fig. 10(a)
shows the corresponding response. In the shown sequence,
the drive operates in the MTPA, field-weakening, current-
limit, and MTPV regions. Spatial-harmonic effects (the sixth
electrical harmonic and its multiples) are most visible in the
actual torque, but also appear in other signals.
2) Real-Time Control Experiment: Fig. 10(b) shows the
corresponding measurement using the same control method as
in the simulation. In addition to the expected sixth electrical
harmonic and its multiples, the measured q-axis current con-
tains first- and fourth-order mechanical harmonics. These low-
order components are likely caused by practical asymmetries
(e.g., eccentricity, ovality, or winding imbalance) not present
in the symmetric FEM model. If necessary, such phenomena
could be incorporated into the proposed model by introducing
corresponding physical variables, such as lateral rotor displace-
ment, as additional inputs.
Additionally, transients appear in the flux reference during
field weakening due to actual DC-bus voltage variations,
whereas the simulation assumed a constant DC-bus voltage.
Otherwise, the simulation and measurement agree well (al-
though the FEM dataset naturally only approximates the real
machine because of uncertainties in geometry and material
properties). Most importantly, the experiment confirms that
the proposed gradient-network model executes seamlessly as
an integral part of the real-time control system.
V. Conclusions
A physics-constrained magnetic modeling framework was
presented for synchronous machines. In this framework, the
magnetic characteristics and electromagnetic torque are mod-
eled directly as gradients of a field energy or co-energy
function. This structure ensures energy conservation and reci-
procity by construction. Spatial harmonics are captured by
encoding the rotor angle through periodic Fourier features.
Monotone gradient networks ensure a unique and invertible
relationship between flux linkages and currents. Symmetrizing
the energy functions enforces q-axis symmetry by construc-
tion. An efficient 𝑝-norm gradient activation reduces the
computational cost of real-time execution. The framework was
validated using measured and FEM datasets, demonstrating
high accuracy and data efficiency. Furthermore, closed-loop
experiments on an embedded platform confirmed its real-time
applicability.
