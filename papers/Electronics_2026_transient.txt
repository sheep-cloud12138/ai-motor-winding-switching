Abstract 
Stator winding reconfiguration technology has attracted increasing attention because it 
can modify the torque–speed output characteristics of motors online and extend the high-
efficiency operating region. However, conventional winding switching methods suffer 
from several limitations, including long transient duration, typically at the millisecond 
level; difficulty in precise control; current interruption or asymmetric operation during 
the transient process; and the resulting torque loss and torque fluctuation. To address 
these issues, this paper first proposes a seamless winding switching method. The core idea 
is to complete the switching within the zero-vector interval of a single SVPWM period, 
thereby eliminating potential surge voltage without requiring a snubber circuit. The pro-
posed method reduces the transient duration to only 5 μs, eliminates current interruption 
and current asymmetry, and suppresses the associated torque ripple. Furthermore, a 
deadbeat predictive control strategy is employed. By directly controlling the voltage vec-
tor, the proposed strategy enables fast and accurate current tracking within one sampling 
period after reconfiguration, thereby avoiding current overshoot, oscillation, and the cor-
responding torque and speed fluctuations. In addition, a voltage feedforward compensa-
tion method is proposed to pre-compensate the voltage applied during the PWM period 
in which the winding reconfiguration is performed, thereby preventing the application of 
voltage vectors that are inconsistent with the actual motor winding connection. With these 
three innovative measures, fast, smooth, and robust winding reconfiguration is achieved. 
The mechanisms and implementation procedures of the proposed strategies are described 
in detail, and their feasibility and effectiveness are verified through experiments. 
Keywords: permanent magnet synchronous motor (PMSM); winding reconfiguration; 
seamless switching; zero-voltage vector (ZVV) interval; deadbeat predictive current  
control (DPCC)

1. Introduction 
To address global environmental challenges and the energy crisis, electric vehicles 
(EVs) are undergoing rapid development [1,2]. At present, most EVs adopt a drive topology 
consisting of a permanent magnet synchronous motor (PMSM) and a single-speed reducer. 
However, under certain operating conditions, it is difficult for this configuration to simultane-
ously satisfy the requirements of high torque at low speed and high power at high speed. 
Stator winding reconfiguration has emerged as a promising solution, as it can change the con-
nection mode of the stator windings through external circuits [3]. This technique enables dy-
namic adjustment of the motor torque–speed output characteristics and effectively extends 
the high-efficiency operating region of the motor while providing performance similar to that 
of a conventional multi-speed transmission [4]. 
According to the differences in winding connection topologies, winding reconfigu-
ration can be classified into several types, including star–delta reconfiguration [5–7], 
tapped-winding reconfiguration [8–10], series–parallel reconfiguration [11–14], and 
phase-shift reconfiguration [15–17]. In addition, hybrid reconfiguration schemes combin-
ing star–delta reconfiguration with other methods have also been reported in the literature 
[18–20]. Among these alternatives, series–parallel reconfiguration has received the broad-
est research attention because of its remarkable transformation ratio, continuous utiliza-
tion of all windings, and relatively simple implementation process. 
The switching devices used in the reconfiguration circuit can be mechanical components 
such as relays or contactors [11,21,22]. Although these devices have the advantages of low cost 
and low conduction loss, they also suffer from slow switching speed, large volume, and lim-
ited life. Therefore, power electronic switches, including IGBTs, MOSFETs, and thyristors, 
have become the preferred choice for reconfigurable winding systems [7–10]. 
The major challenge in winding reconfiguration is how to safely change the winding 
connections during motor operation. Sudden switching can generate surge voltage due to 
abrupt changes in the inductive current, which may damage the reconfiguration switches 
or even the inverter. Although integrating dedicated snubber circuits [23,24] provides an 
effective countermeasure, it inevitably increases the system volume, weight, and cost. 
Currently, the most widely used methods are the zero-current method (ZC) [11,22] and 
the phase-by-phase method (PBP) [13,25]. 
In the ZC method, the three-phase currents must first be reduced to zero, and the 
current is then re-established after winding reconfiguration. Although this method en-
sures the safety of the reconfiguration process, it introduces a new problem: the torque is 
completely interrupted, resulting in a consequent speed drop. The PBP method avoids 
torque interruption by changing the winding connection sequentially at the zero-crossing 
instants of each phase current. However, during this process, the motor operates under 
an asymmetric condition, which still causes torque fluctuation. Moreover, this process re-
quires at least one third of an electrical current period. 
The transition duration of these two methods is usually at the millisecond level and 
is affected by multiple factors. For the ZC method, the influencing factors include the in-
ductance value, current amplitude, and current sampling noise. For the PBP method, the 
key influencing factor is the motor speed. In addition, because thyristors have natural 
commutation characteristics, the PBP method is generally more suitable for applications 
in which thyristors are used as reconfiguration switches. Otherwise, inaccurate zero-
crossing detection may still lead to surge voltage generation. 
For EV applications, when fully-controlled fast-switching power devices such as IG-
BTs, MOSFETs, or even SiC MOSFETs are used as reconfiguration switches [7,9,10,17,25], 
it becomes particularly important to develop a high-performance switching control strat-
egy that is well matched with the hardware.

Deadbeat predictive current control (DPCC) has been widely applied in motor drive con-
trol systems because of its extremely fast dynamic response and excellent steady-state perfor-
mance [26,27]. By calculating the required voltage vector applied to the motor, DPCC can 
achieve tracking of the reference current within a single control period. 
This paper proposes an integrated control strategy for series–parallel winding recon-
figuration of PMSM using fully-controlled power electronic devices as reconfiguration 
switches. The proposed strategy combines a seamless switching method with a DPCC 
scheme that incorporates feedforward voltage compensation. 
The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows. 
A seamless switching method based on the zero-voltage-vector (ZVV) interval is pro-
posed. This method eliminates the potential surge voltage, torque interruption, and asym-
metric operation problems associated with conventional switching methods. Meanwhile, 
the transient duration of winding reconfiguration is reduced to a fixed microsecond-level 
timescale, which is 5 μs in this study. 
DPCC is introduced into the winding reconfiguration control system for the first 
time, and a feedforward voltage compensation method is proposed. Combined with the 
proposed seamless switching method, the strategy enables fast and accurate tracking of 
the step-changed current command within one control period, thereby effectively sup-
pressing current overshoot and oscillation after reconfiguration. 
The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the series–
parallel winding reconfiguration system. Section 3 presents the principles and implemen-
tation details of the proposed strategy. Section 4 shows the experimental results. Section 
5 compares and discusses the characteristics and essential differences between the existing 
winding-reconfiguration control schemes and the proposed method. Finally, Section 6 
concludes the paper.

The proposed seamless switching method based on the ZVV interval refers to per-
forming winding reconfiguration during the interval in which u0 or u7 is applied. If wind-
ing reconfiguration is performed during the u7 interval, there will be at least half a sam-
pling period during which the applied voltage vector does not match the winding con-
nection state of the motor. Therefore, in this study, winding reconfiguration is performed 
during the u0 interval at the beginning of one SVPWM period, as indicated by the orange 
region in Figure 4. 
3.1.2. Mode Decomposition of the Reconfiguration Process 
During winding reconfiguration, the operating states of the system can be divided 
into three modes: series mode, transient mode, and parallel mode. Taking the case in 
which the reference ZVV is located in Sector I and the motor is switched from the series 
mode to the parallel mode as an example, the operating states of the system in the three 
modes are described in detail below. 
Mode 1: As shown in Figure 5, the windings operate in the series mode, where the series 
switches S1–S3 are turned on and the parallel switches P1–P6 are turned off. At the end of the 
previous SVPWM period, the output voltage vector of the inverter switches from u4 to u0. The 
current freewheels through the lower bridge arms of the inverter, the motor windings, and 
switches S1–S3. The current flow direction is indicated by the dashed arrows. This state con-
tinues into the u0 interval at the beginning of the current SVPWM period. 
 
Figure 5. Mode 1—The series mode. 
Mode 2: As shown in Figure 6, the states of the inverter and switches S1–S3 remain 
the same as those in Mode 1, while the parallel switches P1–P6 are switched from off to 
on. This is the transient mode, in which all nine reconfiguration switches are turned on. 
The current freewheels through the lower bridge arms of the inverter, the motor windings, 
and all reconfiguration switches. Although the freewheeling loop changes, the magnitude 
and direction of the current in each winding remain unchanged. Therefore, theoretically, 
no surge voltage or abrupt torque change is generated.

3.1.3. Determination of the Minimum ZVV Interval 
When winding reconfiguration is performed, the duration of the ZVV interval at the 
beginning of the SVPWM period should be no less than the winding reconfiguration tran-
sient time, Ttr. 
This transient time should include the turn-on time (ton) and the turn-off time (toff) of 
the reconfiguration switches, as shown in (1). These parameters can be obtained from the 
device datasheet, while the necessary signal propagation delay and safety margin should 
also be considered. In this work, ton = 3 μs and toff = 2 μs are adopted; therefore, the mini-
mum ZVV interval is set as Ttr = 5 μs. If the motor operates under a high modulation index, 
resulting in an insufficient ZVV duration, it can be directly limited to 5 μs in that specific 
SVPWM period. Since this situation occurs with a very low frequency and affects only one 
SVPWM period each time, its negative influence on system performance can be neglected.

In the winding reconfiguration control system, motor parameters, current references 
and actual phase currents may all experience step changes. If conventional DPCC is di-
rectly adopted for reconfiguration transient control without dedicated modifications, 
torque oscillations or even control instability will occur after reconfiguration. Accordingly, 
a feedforward voltage compensation measure is proposed for DPCC targeting winding 
reconfiguration transient control. 
3.2.2. Implementation of DPCC with Voltage Compensation During  
Winding Reconfiguration

treatments are required after the reconfiguration command is received.

3.3. Overview of the Drive and Control System 
Figure 11 shows the block diagram of the winding-reconfigurable motor drive and 
control system investigated in this study. The speed loop adopts conventional PI control, 
while the current loop adopts DPCC. In the reconfiguration algorithm, the parameters 
that need to be updated according to the winding reconfiguration command include the 
motor parameters, namely resistance, inductance, and flux linkage, and the control pa-
rameters, namely the speed-loop PI parameters and the q-axis current gain. Theoretically, 
the speed-loop PI parameters in the series mode are 0.5 times those in the parallel mode 
[28]. Since the output of the speed-loop integrator cannot change in a stepwise manner, a 
q-axis current gain selector is introduced in the current loop to rapidly change the current 
command after winding reconfiguration.

5. Discussion 
The two conventional transient control methods most widely used for series-parallel 
winding reconfiguration are the ZC method [11,22] and the PBP method [13,25]. This sec-
tion elaborates on the core characteristics and critical distinctions between these two clas-
sical approaches and the ZVV-DPCC scheme proposed in this work. Table 5 lists the re-
spective characteristics of the three methods. 
By comparison, the following conclusions can be drawn. The ZC method has the 
broadest applicability, as it is compatible with all types of reconfiguration switches. How-
ever, complete torque interruption and the consequent speed drop render it inadequate 
for high-performance electric drive systems. The PBP method is suitable for applications 
adopting thyristors as reconfiguration switches. It eliminates torque interruption yet in-
troduces brief asymmetric operation, which still generates torque and speed fluctuations. 
Both the ZC and PBP methods feature millisecond-scale transient durations and are sus-
ceptible to motor operating conditions, sampling accuracy and other factors. The pro-
posed ZVV-DPCC scheme is applicable to systems utilizing fully-controlled power elec-
tronic devices as reconfiguration switches. It achieves microsecond-level, precisely con-
trollable transients and eliminates torque and speed fluctuations.

6. Conclusions 
This paper proposes an integrated control strategy for a series–parallel winding re-
configuration system using fully-controlled power electronic devices as reconfiguration 
switches. The proposed strategy combines a smooth winding switching method based on 
the ZVV interval with deadbeat predictive current control incorporating voltage feedfor-
ward compensation. 
The proposed strategy effectively addresses a series of key issues associated with 
conventional winding switching methods, including long transient process, surge voltage, 
current interruption, torque and speed fluctuations, and asymmetric operating states. 


===== PAGE 17 =====

Electronics 2026, 15, 3120 
17 of 19 
 
https://doi.org/10.3390/electronics15143120 
The superior control performance of the proposed method is predicated on the prem-
ise that accurate motor parameters are obtained in advance through offline parameter 
identification. However, in practice, motor parameters vary with operating conditions. 
Therefore, enhancing the global robustness of DPCC against motor parameter variations 
remains a critical topic for further research. Future work will explore the integration of 
advanced control algorithms, such as online parameter identification, disturbance ob-
server-based compensation, and model-free predictive control. 
Another potential concern is that during the reconfiguration transient, simultaneous 
conduction of all series and parallel switches may give rise to circulating currents caused 
by asymmetries including winding manufacturing deviations, inconsistent voltage drops 
across power devices, and mismatched drive signal delays. Mitigating this latent issue 
also constitutes a valuable research direction.
